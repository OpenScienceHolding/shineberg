People ask for an advisor, a consultant, or a strategic lead as if I sold three different things. I don't. An advisor recommends and owns nothing, and a consultant runs a defined project with an end. The ongoing work is one format: ongoing advisor or part-time strategic lead, who holds strategy and go-to-market over time and is measured by decisions, not hours.

## When each one fits

**An advisor** fits when you want a recommendation and you will own what happens next. The advisor does not hold the plan, the go-to-market, or the call. You take the advice, or you don't, and the work stays yours.

**A consultant** fits when the work has an edge you can already see. You can name the project, the scope, and the end. When that project is done, the consultant is done. A bounded piece of work is a real need. It is just not an open seat.

**Ongoing advisor or part-time strategic lead** fits when nobody is holding strategy and go-to-market, and the venture still has to move. I hear that in a few shapes, and they are not separate offers. A founder has an idea and some experience, and does not yet know the concrete first step. The venture is moving, and nobody is holding strategy and go-to-market. A senior executive is facing AI disruption or a market shift, and needs to decide where to move first. Same format. The question is whether someone has to hold the strategy over time, not which title sounds senior.

The questions that show up are concrete. Is this idea worth my next year? What is weak in the plan before more money goes in? We have traction: what do we focus on, and what do we stop? Where does AI change the market, and where is it noise? If you can carry the answer yourself, you may want an advisor. If the work has a date when it ends, you may want a consultant. If the answer has to be held while the venture moves, that is the ongoing format.

If what you need is AI systems built, that is not this work. That is [Reef TRH](https://reeftrh.com), my agency with Raviv: diagnosis first, then systems that deliver return.

## What the format looks like with me

On [strategy consulting](/strategy), this is the third way in. Next to it sit a single deep session and a defined engagement. The ongoing line is plain: I hold strategy and go-to-market with you over time. That can be advisory, equity, or a combination.

It is not a deck, and it is not a report. A decision, not a report. Each engagement ends at a decision point agreed up front. If I don't know, I say so, and we turn the gaps into a discovery plan instead of invented numbers. I would rather show a rough truth than a polished guess.

When the work needs a written artifact, it is an opportunity map: where to move, where to wait, and what to drop. The plan has to survive execution. I have founded and built ventures myself, from market research in 2009 through Mela, Feezback, and lezel, and I still build. I push back when something sounds generic. That is not friction. It is how we get to the real thing. I do not publish client stories. The work is personal. If you want a check, I can introduce you to someone I have worked with, after we talk and only if they agree, and I will sign an NDA before you share anything.

Every format starts the same way: scope before work. Before anything starts, three things go on the table: a defined scope, a decision point, and clear terms, in writing. There is no fixed pricing. It depends on scope, stage, equity, and depth. What is fixed is those three things, not a rate card. A clear message beats a meeting. If we meet, it is to decide something, and you come with the purpose in one line.

## How to start

Do not start by picking a title for me. Start with one concrete question.

The usual way in is a [45-minute working session](/book) on one concrete question. Come with the purpose in one line. You leave with an honest read and a next step.

That session has three parts. What you bring: one concrete question, in one line. What I'll ask: what you've tried, what's at stake, what would change your mind. What you leave with: an honest read and one next step, or a clear "not with me."

A clear message beats a meeting. If we meet, it is to decide something. Times are shown in your time zone. [Book](/book) also lists a 20-minute intro, and a Reef TRH diagnosis scoping call if the company needs AI systems built. Those are different meetings. The 45-minute working session is the one that starts this.

If one session is enough, we stop. If it makes sense to go further, we scope it together. When the work has an end, that is a defined engagement: multi-month, with a defined scope, a decision point, and clear terms, in writing, before work starts. The ongoing format is the one where I stay and hold strategy and go-to-market. We do not pick that from a menu in the first line of an email.

## FAQ

### Do you work for equity?

Sometimes, for longer partnerships - advisory, equity, or a combination. We put it on the table early.

### Can we start with a single meeting?

Yes. A 45-minute working session on one concrete question is the usual way in. If it makes sense to go further, we scope it together.

### What does this cost?

No fixed pricing - it depends on scope, stage, equity and depth. What's fixed: every engagement starts with a defined scope, a decision point and clear terms.

### Is a strategic lead a different offer from an ongoing advisor?

No. Ongoing advisor or part-time strategic lead is one format. I hold strategy and go-to-market with you over time. The two names are not two products.

### How is this different from a consultant on a project?

A consultant runs a defined project with an end. This format does not. It holds strategy and go-to-market over time, and it is measured by decisions, not hours. If what you need is a bounded project, say so. We will scope a defined engagement, or we will not start.
